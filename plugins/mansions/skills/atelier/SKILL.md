# Atelier

A design studio in one skill: a brief, a dealt design, pages built in HTML, rendered and measured by scripts, then judged by a critic who did not make them. Sources and licences: LICENSE and CREDITS.md in this folder, and vendor/MANIFEST.md.

**The core belief.** Removing the tells of AI design is not enough: a page with every cliché removed and nothing put in its place is empty and polite. Every piece needs a **material idea** (something that belongs to this subject, made visible), a **direction** dealt on purpose, and **proof** that it works, measured on the rendered output, not assumed.

`S` below is this skill's folder. Run every script by its absolute path from the user's project; compose.py prints the absolute path of every script it names, and `compose.py <command> --help` explains each command. compose.py keeps the project's memory in `.atelier/` and finds the nearest one at or above the folder it runs in, so it also works from a page's own folder. Renders and check outputs are written next to the input file unless you pass `--out`.

## Quick path: one image about X
Use this for a single image or a one-off post. Quick scope has four stages (atoms, angles, genome, visual), and only the visual stage needs an independent critic. `python S/scripts/compose.py next <slug>` prints the exact next command at any point. Each grade takes a notes file with one line per criterion, "criterion: reason", the reason 25 characters or more; the worksheet prints the template. compose.py checks that length, not that the reason quotes or locates evidence: write a true one.
1. `python S/scripts/compose.py new <slug> --topic "X" --scope quick --medium image` (add `--brand <tokens.css>` for the user's brand; it goes to the palette line). A slug uses lower case letters, digits, dots, dashes and underscores.
2. Write 3 to 5 atoms in 01-atoms.md: real facts, numbers or objects, each with its source (a URL or a published work; `first-party: <file>` for the user's own record). Never invent a number. The genome deal reads this table: a hook or close that needs material (a confession needs a first-party atom, a moment a dated instant, a quote a real line) is dealt only when a sourced atom carries it. Grade them: `compose.py grade <slug> atoms --scores "coverage=N,specificity=N,sourced=N,surprise=N" --notes atoms.md --by self`
3. `compose.py deal <slug> angles`, pick the ONE dealt operator that fits the atoms, write ONE angle with it, grade it (`grade <slug> angles ... --by self`), then `compose.py choose <slug> angle <n> --text "<one line>"`, where n is that operator's number.
4. `compose.py deal <slug> genome`: it deals ONE direction plus register, type, colour harmony, hook and layout, away from the pieces in `.atelier/history.jsonl` (`--pin axis=value` fixes an axis first, for example `--pin register=warmth`). 04-genome.md says what the dealt hook and close mean, and how to compose the page: the direction's grammar, the specimen frame to leave, each page's layout word, the object atom and the word budget. Grade it (`grade <slug> genome --scores "coherence=N,feasibility=N" ... --by self`).
5. Run the `palette.py` line printed in 04-genome.md, in the page's folder (it writes palette.css; `next` prints this line first while palette.css is missing), then `compose.py scaffold <slug>`: a visual.html with absolute links to tokens.css, fonts and palette.css. It is a composition start, not the specimen: the grid, the dealt type and the palette.css roles (the direction's own colour tokens follow them too), with page 1 set up for its dealt layout word, which is never the specimen's own layout. The specimen's frame and sample words stay out of the page, because the art-director judges distinct against that specimen. Quick scope deals only directions that have a specimen, so there is always a picture of the grammar to read: take its rules, and build the direction's device out of the topic's own object, in the dealt layout. The specimen's stock parts (a ring station, a hairline under a numeral, a stack of rhombi; data/directions.json "stock", listed in 04-genome.md and 07-visual.md) must not appear unchanged, because critics read them as the specimen itself. Scaffold links the `new --brand` file after tokens.css, and fills the logo slot from the brand file's `--logo` (`--logo-on-dark` on a dark or colour-field ground) or removes it, printing why. Keep the `data-role` attributes, and mark the user's logo `data-role="logo"`. Keep the page to 45 words, the hook to 8 (references/copy.md "Word budget").
6. `node S/scripts/render.mjs visual.html --audit`: writes page-01.png and audit.json, and fails on any hard rule (type floors measured as drawn, contrast, overlap, a rule or border running through text, text off the page, a broken link, a missing image, a fallback font). AI-look tells (among them fake product chrome, pill tags, too many corner radii, panels nested three deep and raw colour values outside the tokens), shapes painted over text and a page over the 45-word budget only warn. Five more warnings name what the art-director charged in the 30 Oct 2026 runs: `phone read` (text under 36 px, or under 40 px in a weight under 500, fine print included; the page line gives the smallest size with fine print), `claim twice` (a line that repeats the hook's words), `wrapped run` (identical marks that wrap and leave a short last row), `hairline accent` (an accent mark drawn under 8 px) and `empty cell` (a cell mostly empty beside a full one); two more, `faint mark` (a mark with no text under 3:1 against its ground) and `thin stroke` (display type whose strokes are under 2 px on a phone), name what the second run charged. Fix every FAIL and those seven, and cut to the budget, before any critic sees the page.
7. `python S/scripts/attention.py <folder>`: page 1 must be led by the hook, the number or the object (rule under "Hard rules"). Exit 2 means it found no page PNGs: render first.
8. Write the post text that goes out with the image (post.md next to the page): every claim, number and quote on the page is in it. One art-director pass on the rendered PNG and the post text (see "Critics" below), saved to a verdict file. It scores exactly the rubric's visual criteria (idea, hook, hierarchy, craft, colour, distinct; idea and hook weigh 1.5) and ends with the grade line. Then `compose.py grade <slug> visual --scores "<the critic's scores>" --notes visual.md --by critic --evidence <verdict file>`. compose.py refuses scores that differ from the verdict file and recomputes the total; a FAIL never passes and counts as one of 3 attempts. After a FAIL, `next` names the best attempt so far, kept with its page, renders and verdict in `.atelier/pieces/<slug>/attempts/visual-<n>`: start again from that one (copy its visual.html back if a later attempt scored lower), apply only its verdict's numbered fixes, keep what its `keep:` line names, add nothing else, re-render, re-check, and give the new render to a fresh art-director. compose.py checks the verdict's form, scores and binding, not who wrote it, so the verdict must come from the art-director. The grade is bound to this piece: the render in the folder scaffold wrote (or `--folder` with this piece's own visual.html), page-NN.png and a PASS audit.json newer than visual.html, a verdict headed `## <slug>`, and a verdict file no other piece used.
9. `compose.py deliver <slug>` remembers the piece (scope quick still counts for diversity) and adds the cover to the layout memory. Hand over the files.

Run the full pipeline below for a carousel or a series, for client work, and for anything published under a brand where a weak angle costs more than the extra stages.

## Pick the mode
Read the ask and the platform, then pick one mode. Every mode runs `render.mjs --audit`, `copy_lint.py` and `cvd.py`, plus its own checks below. If the ask fits two modes, take the one the platform plays (a LinkedIn PDF is a carousel, a talk is slides); if it is still unclear, ask one question.
| The ask or platform | Mode and reference | Size | Its own checks | Critics |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| one image, a feed post, an infographic | single image: the quick path above | 1080x1350 (4:5); 1080x1080; 1600x900 for X | `attention.py`, `layout_memory.py check` | art-director |
| a poster: one claim with its proof | poster: an image dealt format `i-poster` | 1080x1350 for a feed; print sizes, bleed and resolution are not checked | as a single image | art-director |
| a swipe post, a LinkedIn document | carousel: the full pipeline, references/carousel.md | 1080x1350 a page, `render.mjs --pdf` | `attention.py` on every page, `layout_memory.py`, `contact_sheet.py` | story-editor, copy-critic, art-director |
| a talk, a pitch, a shared screen | slides: references/slides.md | 1920x1080 | `render.mjs --size 1920x1080 --pdf`, `deck_check.py`, `attention.py` | story-editor, copy-critic, art-director |
| a story or a reel cover | story: references/carousel.md (UI safe zones) | 1080x1920, `--safe reels` | as a single image | art-director |
| a short film for a muted feed | motion film: references/motion.md; a drawn film with a camera, references/art-motion.md | 1080x1350 or 1080x1920 | `motion.mjs`, `render.mjs --audit --seconds N`, `video_check.py`, `attention.py` on frame0.png | art-director on contact.png and phone.png |

## Critics
Three critic agents ship with the plugin: **story-editor** (atoms, angles, sections), **art-director** (rendered images, never code) and **copy-critic** (the words). In a plugin install their names are `atelier:story-editor`, `atelier:art-director` and `atelier:copy-critic`. The **humanizer** skill rewrites long copy.
- In every critic prompt, give the absolute paths of `S/references/critique.md`, `S/references/tells.md` and `S/data/rubrics.json` (and `S/references/copy.md` for the copy-critic), plus the piece's slug (the verdict's `## <slug>` heading), the files to judge, the post text and the script output as measured facts, and for the art-director the dealt direction and the brief's material idea line (for its `reads as:` line). Do not give your reasons or what you think you fixed.
- Save each verdict to a new file for this piece. A stage that compose.py marks as critic-graded needs that file: `compose.py grade <slug> <stage> --scores "<the critic's scores>" ... --by critic --evidence <verdict file>`. Type the critic's scores into `--scores`; compose.py refuses numbers that differ from the verdict file and recomputes the total. A verdict that says FAIL or REVISE never passes. Every grade records the sha256 of the verdict file; a passing visual grade also records visual.html and the pages. compose.py checks the verdict's form, scores and binding, not who wrote it.
- Before relying on a critic you changed, calibrate it (references/critique.md). One run each is logged in evals/anchors and evals/angles (29 Oct 2026, all flaws caught, all strong items passed), before the art-director and references/critique.md changed on 30 Oct. The art-director was calibrated again after those changes (30 Oct 2026, Sonnet, 7 of 7 flaws caught, 3 of 3 strong passed; verdicts in evals/anchors/runs/2026-10-30).

## The full pipeline (each step has a gate)
Run it through the composer: `compose.py` deals lenses, play operators and the genome, remembers every delivered piece in `.atelier/history.jsonl` (the one piece memory), and refuses to advance a stage until it is graded at or above its threshold with evidence (references/composer.md). Details per step: references/process.md.
1. **Intake.** Format, size, platform, audience, the one thing the viewer should remember, deadline, brand. If the brand is unknown, build a brand file first (references/brand.md). Never guess a brand from memory.
2. **Material.** Gather real material before designing: the product's real screens, the real numbers, the real words, the real logo. Never redraw a product UI from imagination. Never invent a number, quote, client or result; sample content is labelled as sample.
3. **Brief (Gate 1).** Write `brief.md`: the one-line idea, the material idea ("On these pages, [something that belongs to this subject] becomes [the direction's device]"), the audience, what the feed already looks like, and the one novel element. For an art piece with few words, first name its movement in two words (references/brief.md). Run the **swap test**: put a competitor's name on it; if it still works for them, it is generic. Start again.
4. **Deal the design (Gate 2).** `compose.py deal <slug> genome` deals ONE direction with the register, type, harmony, arc, hook, close and layout rhythm, weighted away from `.atelier/history.jsonl` and at least a set number of axes away from each of the last 10 pieces. Write one sentence on how the material lives in that direction. If the direction fights the material, deal again (the next deal excludes it). Do not pick a direction by hand.
5. **Thumbnails.** For a cover or a key frame, build 3 to 6 quick variants in the dealt direction and pick the one strongest at 360 px wide. Only then build every page.
6. **Build and measure (Gate 3).** Start from `compose.py scaffold`: for an image or a carousel it writes a composition start on the grid, each page set up for its dealt layout word, never the specimen's page. In full and design scope, a dealt direction without a specimen gives a plain start, and scaffold says so: build that direction from references/directions.md. Pages are `<section class="page">`; motion is a pure function of time with `window.seek(t)`. Mark `data-role` on the hook, the number or object, any accent, the logo and fine print. Then run the scripts in the table above. Every FAIL is fixed before anyone reviews. The scripts state facts; you do not argue with them.
7. **Critique (Gate 4).** An independent critic reviews the rendered images, never the code (references/critique.md). Scores with caps; the three worst problems, each with a concrete fix; one thing to keep.
8. **Fix the three worst, re-render, re-measure.** At most three rounds. Before keeping a revision, compare it with the best earlier render in both orders: `pairwise.py prepare <best.png> <new.png> --out <dir>`, a fresh art-director for each sheet, then `pairwise.py judge`; keep the new one only if it wins in both orders. If it is still under the bar, say so plainly and offer a simpler format.
9. **Deliver.** Files (PNG pages, PDF for document carousels, MP4 with poster frame and captions), the contact sheet, alt text, the material idea in one line, and what you would improve with more time. `compose.py deliver <slug>` appends the piece to `.atelier/history.jsonl` and adds the cover to the layout memory. In design scope (`--scope design`, inside a host system), deliver only after the host has approved the piece.

## Hard rules
- Evidence before claims: never say a piece is done, readable or on-brand without the rendered output and the script results in front of you.
- One subject, one path for the eye, one claim per page or shot. One accent, under 5% of the area, on the one thing to remember (render.mjs --audit measures the accent paint against palette.css's --accent, text left out, and warns at 5%; a filled row or band is the usual cause).
- Type on a 1080 px wide canvas: 32 px or more (36 px for condensed faces), measured as drawn after SVG and CSS transforms; only labelled fine print (`data-role="fine"`) goes smaller, never under 22 px. Contrast 4.5:1 for text under 48 px, 3:1 above. The hook is the biggest type on the cover.
- Attention (attention.py, by each element's share of the page's saliency): page 1 is led by the hook, the number or the object. A logo, or an element without a `data-role`, never leads (FAIL). An accent may lead by under 1.5 times the best hook, number or object, which prints a note; by more, it fails. Body and fine text are left out of the lead race. An accent inside a hook or number box counts as that element.
- Motion: frame 0 already carries the hook (feeds autoplay muted); captions of 7 words or fewer, on screen long enough to read; something new every 2 to 4 seconds; springs, not linear moves; no timers or Math.random in render code; end on a hold, not a fade.
- No AI-look defaults (references/tells.md), including the palettes generated design clusters around (cream near #F4F1EA with a serif and terracotta; near-black with one acid accent; without `--brand`, a paper ground is never dealt a serif display face) unless they are the real brand: centred title on a gradient, everything fading in, glows, glassmorphism, neon, brains, robots, sparkles, tiny uppercase labels above every heading, 01/02/03 on things that are not steps, fake dashboards of grey bars.
- Replace, never just remove: every ornament removed is replaced by scale, real material or a full colour field.
- Copy on the design obeys references/copy.md: no selling, no preaching, no cringe, no engagement bait; precise numbers only from real sources. No headline, quote or claim on a page that the post text does not carry.
- Respect licences: fonts, images and music need a licence that allows the use. The default tokens.css is a neutral palette; The Mansions Agency's brand is an optional theme (assets/themes/mansions.css, with its logo), loaded after tokens.css or passed with `--brand`. Its logo is a trademark of The Mansions Agency: use it only for work by or for that agency, and the user's own logo otherwise.

## Output contract
Every delivery states: the material idea, the direction and why, the check results (audit, attention, layout memory, colour vision, video check), the critique scores per round, the files, and alt text. If a check could not run (no Playwright, no ffmpeg), say which and that the work is unverified.
