# Colour

## A palette is proportions, not swatches (taste-skill)
- Ground: 70 to 90% of the area. Text and structure: most of the rest. Accent: under 5%, on ONE thing per page, the thing to remember. The accent is never on every heading or every border.
- The "60-30-10 rule" is a decorating heuristic, not research; use the proportions above and judge by the render.
- Separate surfaces with a lightness step of 2 to 6 points, not with lines.
- Rotate grounds across a set or a feed: dark, paper, a full brand-colour field, a warm field. Two pieces in a row on the same ground start to look like a template.

## Colour is relative (Josef Albers, "Interaction of Color", 1963)
The same colour looks different on different grounds and next to different neighbours. Never judge a colour in a swatch; judge it in the render, on its ground, at phone size. A brand teal that sings on cream can die on near-black; give it a lighter step for dark grounds (the `--teal-lit` role in a brand file is that step).

## Contrast families (after Johannes Itten, "The Art of Color", 1961)
Itten's seven contrasts are a checklist for making the accent work: light-dark (the strongest and the safest), warm-cool, complementary, saturation (a saturated accent among muted colours), extension or quantity (a small amount of a strong colour balances a large amount of a weak one; this is why the accent stays under 5%), simultaneous (neighbours shift each other), and hue. Use one or two deliberately; using all of them is noise.

## Legibility and access
- WCAG 2.2 AA: text under about 24 px CSS (18 pt) needs 4.5:1 against its ground; large text 3:1. On social pages the audit uses: under 3:1 fails, under 4.5:1 fails for text under 48 px, under 7:1 warns for body text. Body text at 7:1 or more reads well on a dim phone.
- Never encode meaning in hue alone; add position, a label or a shape. `python scripts/cvd.py <folder>` shows each page as protan, deutan and tritan viewers see it (Machado, Oliveira and Fernandes 2009) and warns when an accent or hook blends into the ground. About 1 in 12 men and 1 in 200 women have a colour-vision deficiency.
- For data colours, use a colour-blind-safe set such as Okabe and Ito's (orange #E69F00, sky blue #56B4E9, bluish green #009E73, yellow #F0E442, blue #0072B2, vermillion #D55E00, reddish purple #CC79A7) or one brand accent plus greys (references/dataviz.md).
- Check captions over moving video at the brightest point where they sit, not on the first frame; use a neutral scrim (black at 40 to 55%) when needed, never a gradient fog.

## Connotation by system (palette.py, data/colour.json)
Colour meaning is assigned on purpose, per piece, from the register the composer dealt, not picked by taste on the day:
- **Lightness carries pleasantness and seriousness.** Brighter colours are rated more pleasant, darker ones more dominant (Valdez and Mehrabian 1994): a dark ground reads serious and focused, a light one open and calm.
- **Chroma carries energy.** More saturated colours are rated more arousing (same study): raise chroma on ONE element for energy; lower it everywhere for calm or rigour.
- **Temperature carries distance.** Warm reads near, active, human; cool reads far, calm, technical (a design convention with mixed experimental support: use it, do not cite it as law).
- **Value span carries drama.** A wide range of lights and darks reads dramatic; a narrow one, quiet.
- **Hue carries learned meaning,** which varies by culture and context. Shared patterns exist across 30 nations, shaped by language and geography (Jonauskaite et al. 2020), but the brand teaches its own meaning. Keep the brand's hues; move lightness, chroma and proportion.

Each register has a recipe (data/colour.json "recipes"): wonder is a deep ground with one luminous, high-chroma accent; dry is a restricted neutral with a tiny low-chroma accent; relief is the dawn arc (dark pages giving way to light); unease-to-control starts warmer and more saturated and ends cool and calm; curiosity is a neutral field with one complementary clue; rigour is a monochrome value scale.

**Harmonies** (computed in OKLCH, a perceptually even colour space): monochrome, analogous, complementary, split, triadic, each with when to use it and its risk. **Arcs** across a carousel or film, like a film's colour script: steady, dawn, cooldown, single-flash (one full accent page at the peak), chapters. A bookend set opens and closes as one family: the cover and the last page share the deep ground and the middle pages run light. palette.py has no bookend arc, so set the two end pages' ground by hand and audit again.

### What palette.py does

`python scripts/palette.py --register relief --harmony analogous --arc dawn --pages 6 --ground dark` writes palette.css (one class per page) and colour-plan.md (the reasoning, every page's contrast, the colour-vision check and the warnings), and exits 1 if a page is unreadable or an accent vanishes for protan, deutan or tritan vision. The warnings below never change the exit code.

- **The brand's hues are the only hues.** It reads every colour custom property in the brand file (`--brand <tokens.css>`; without it, see "Own palette" below). A colour with OKLCH chroma under 0.02 is a tinted neutral (paper, ink, steel) and is left out; the rest are grouped into hue families (hues within 25 degrees of the next one form a family, and a family may stretch 8 degrees past its outermost colour). The accent comes from `--house`. A brand file without `--house` uses the colour it names primary, brand or accent, else its most chromatic colour, and one NOTE line says which token it used. A brand file with no chromatic colour at all gets a neutral grey accent (no hue is added) and a NOTE. Each harmony hue (the harmony's offset from the accent) is replaced by the nearest family's hue, so a teal brand with an `--amber` gets amber as its complement and never an invented plum or pink. If the brand has one family only, accent-2 is a quieter step (lower chroma, less contrast) of that hue, and the plan says which hues it used. Only `--taste` adds hues, because a taste palette carries one accent hue; the output then says "taste palette: accent-2 hue ... is new".
- **Temperature (arc cooldown, or a register whose recipe is warm-to-cool-arc).** The warm end is the brand's own warm family when it has one (hue within 45 degrees of amber, 70). With none, the accent leans at most 15 degrees toward amber inside its own family. Either way it never passes through the green band (OKLCH hue 105 to 172) unless the brand has a green family itself, because turning teal toward amber the short way goes through mint and the arc then never reads warm. Warmth also comes from lightness and chroma: the warm end is 0.10 lighter on a dark ground (0.10 deeper on paper), and on the cooldown arc its chroma is 1.25 times the register's, falling to 0.65 times at the calm end, where lightness is back to normal. The arc moves in at most three acts (warm, middle, calm); pages inside one act share an accent, because a ramp over nine pages changes less than the eye can see from one page to the next. With a separate warm family only the first act is warm and the other acts take the brand hue, with no hue ramp between them.
- **Own palette (no `--brand`).** A piece without a brand file gets a palette made for it, not the specimens' colours. In the 30 Oct 2026 runs of the README example every unbranded page wore the specimens' own paper #f1f2f4, ink #1c1e23 and a blue 0.05 OKLab from their #143ca1, and the critics read each page as the specimen. Now the register gives the accent a hue band and the neutrals a hue (data/colour.json "own": wonder a luminous cyan over a deep blue-green night, craft-pride a ledger green on a pale khaki stock, curiosity one magenta clue on its complement, a sage grey, and so on). The dealt harmony and arc pick the place in the band, and the harmony adds its other hues freely, as a taste palette does. The paper, night, ink and cream are tinted neutrals at the neutrals' hue: each one takes chroma up to 0.016 (0.032 for the night and the ink) and then steps in lightness until it is 0.04 OKLab or more from the specimen colour in its role (paper, night and the field ground against theirs, ink and cream against theirs) and out of the clusters below. Each accent is moved the least way (lightness down by 0.05 or 0.10, or the hue turned up to 60 degrees) until it is 0.10 or more from every specimen accent (#143ca1, #2f5bc2, #75a3f9, #f8c153, #fff6e8). 0.04 is twice the just-noticeable difference, as for the arc step below; 0.10 makes the accent another colour, not a shade of theirs. Both numbers are our choice. The plan prints one NOTE with the colours it made. Tested over every register, harmony, arc and ground (tests/test_palette.py, check P11) and over 20 dealt genomes (tests/test_composer.py, `r11_tests`).
- **Clear of the clusters, and an accent that is not ink (no `--brand`).** An own ground keeps SPEC_MIN (0.04) past a cluster's edge and 15 degrees past its hue range, not only outside it: in the second 30 Oct 2026 run the warm paper #efe4dc sat 0.034 from cluster 1's cream (radius 0.03), one degree under its hue range, and the critic read it as warm cream. It is now #e3d8d1, 0.070 from the cream. An own accent keeps 0.20 OKLab (ACC_TEXT, our choice) from its page's text at 4.5:1 or more on the ground: that run's madder accent sat 0.160 from its brown ink and read as ink, while accents 0.221 and 0.239 from theirs read as accents. Lightness moves first (up on paper, down on a dark ground); a plan that cannot reach it prints a WARN. Tested in tests/test_palette.py (checks P13 and P14).
- **Warm neutrals and a warm accent (register warmth, recipe warm-neutrals: "paper, warmed", analogous, accent chroma 0.05 to 0.10, temperature warm).** Without `--brand` the own palette of warmth is warm throughout: its neutrals are tinted at 58 to 70 degrees (a warm buff, kept out of cluster 1) and its accent is a madder red at 16 to 28 degrees, chroma 0.09, with the analogous neighbour nearer amber as accent-2. An earlier version warmed only the neutrals and left a cool navy accent (#214e82). With `--brand` or `--taste` the palette's own paper, ink, night and hues are kept, and a NOTE says so. Tested in tests/test_palette.py (checks P10 and P12).
- **A visible arc step is checked.** Where one act gives way to the next, the two accents must differ by an OKLab distance of at least 0.04, or the plan prints "WARN arc steps too small to see". The threshold is twice the just-noticeable difference of 0.02 that CSS Color 4 uses for deltaEOK, because pages are seen one after another, not side by side. The factor of 2 is our choice, not a measured value. A page that is a field page is skipped, as it carries its own colour.
- **The register can override the harmony.** A register whose recipe is monochrome (wonder, dry, craft-pride, rigour) keeps its accent to a sliver on one thing, so a second accent colour has no place to go. When the dealt harmony is something else, the plan prints and records one line, "harmony complementary overridden by register dry: recipe restricted-neutral is monochrome, accent < 3% of the area, so one accent hue applies and there is no accent-2", and palette.css has no `--accent-2`. Other recipes keep the dealt harmony.
- **Clusters.** It compares each page's ground and accent with the two colour clusters of references/tells.md (stored in data/colour.json "clusters"). A ground within 0.03 OKLab of the warm cream #f4f1ea and warm in hue (OKLCH hue 60 to 110, some chroma, so a cool paper or a neutral white does not count) is cluster 1 even without the clay accent, because palette.py cannot see the type face and the cluster is cream plus a serif; the warning says to keep off the serif and the clay accent. A near-black ground (within 0.06 of #0b0b0b) is cluster 2 only together with an acid-green or vermilion accent. Without `--brand` this prints "WARN"; with `--brand` the palette is the user's own, so it prints "NOTE ... matches a common cluster; fine if it is your brand".
- `--ground` is the direction's ground from the genome (a departure board is dark, a ledger is paper); without it the register chooses. The arc then moves away from it: dawn always goes from dark to paper, chapters cycles, single-flash puts one field page at about 60% of the way.
- `--field-page N` (1-based; several numbers or the flag repeated work too) makes page N the full-colour field page for any arc, which is how the genome's "field" layout word gets its colour. With it, single-flash puts its one field page on N instead of its own choice; chapters and the others keep their grounds and add N. Without it nothing changes: only single-flash and chapters have a field page. A number outside 1 to `--pages` is refused. An unknown `--register`, `--harmony` or `--arc`, a missing `--brand` file and `--pages` under 1 stop with a one-line error and exit 2; exit 1 means a page needs a fix.
- `--taste <id>` starts from one of taste-skill's 56 curated palettes (`--list-taste` lists them) instead of the brand.

**Applying it.** Link palette.css after the direction's styles, give each page its class (`<section class="page lg pg-1">`), and point the direction's roles at the planned colours: its ground at `var(--ground)`, its text at `var(--text)` and `var(--text-2)`, its accent at `var(--accent)` and, when the plan has one, `var(--accent-2)` (a plan whose register overrode the harmony has none: use no second accent). Then audit again. Every colour on the page comes from a token: a hex, rgb or hsl value written into the direction's rules (like `#2e2c2840`) does not move when the palette or the ground changes, so point it at `var(--text)` or another role too. `render.mjs --audit` warns on raw colour values outside the tokens. One thing the render audit will not catch: a logo made for the other ground (use the dark lockup on paper and the white one on dark grounds, or it disappears; attention.py fails a logo under 3:1 against its ground). Tested on the ledger specimen, turned from paper to the dawn arc's dark first page: the audit passed and the dark logo vanished, which is why that line is here.

## What colour does not do
Popular claims like "blue means trust" or "red increases urgency" have weak, context-dependent evidence. Colour meaning is learned from the brand and the category. Use colour for structure, isolation and contrast, and let the brand teach the meaning by consistency.

## Dark and light feeds
Most social apps are used in both modes. A dark piece is a pattern break in a light feed and calm in a dark one; a paper piece the reverse. Alternating grounds across a series covers both.

## Banned colour moves
Purple-to-blue gradients, neon on black, glows behind text, glassmorphism, gradient text, a different accent on every page, pure #000 with pure #FFF for body text on large areas (harsh; use near-black and off-white).
