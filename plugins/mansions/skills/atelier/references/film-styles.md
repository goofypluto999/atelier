# Film styles: one look for a whole drawn film

Use this when a drawn film (references/art-motion.md) needs a look. Pick one style for the whole film. Every style here is written in the same eleven parts, so a new one can be added in the same form.

Source: the eleven-part schema and the eight styles below are rewritten from lemo-opuscar by LemoLab (MIT, commit bebbbe1; its styles/_template/STYLE.md and the STYLE.md of swiss-motion, whiteboard, dataviz, pictogram-motion, risograph, silkscreen-poster, one-line and halftone-dossier). No code, demo, font, palette or film of it is copied. The prose is rewritten in Atelier's words; a few short phrases and terms match the source. Its references to living artists and active studios are left out, and no style here imitates one.

## The eleven parts
1. **Essence**: three to five traits that tell a viewer which style this is from one frame, then what it is not.
2. **Material**: the real-world medium, and how the code fakes it.
3. **Colour logic**: rules for the dealt colour plan (palette.css: `--ground`, `--text`, `--text-2`, `--accent`, `--accent-2`), never a fixed palette.
4. **Words on screen**: type roles and where words live. Words stay HTML with their `data-role` and `data-read` (references/motion.md); words drawn in the canvas are reported with `window.texts(t)`.
5. **Motion quality**: easing, stepping, how things arrive and leave, what never moves.
6. **Camera grammar**: the moves from art-motion.md that suit the medium, and what each one says.
7. **Rhythm without sound**: feeds play muted first, so the rhythm must be visible.
8. **Native moves**: moments only this medium can do, each with LinkedIn content it fits. Use one, at the peak.
9. **Pitfalls**: mistakes that come from the medium, each with its fix.
10. **Build in Atelier**: what is canvas, what is HTML, which helpers do it.
11. **Variation space**: three structures, three openings, three endings, all far from any example.

The rules in references/motion.md and art-motion.md hold for every style: frame 0 carries the hook, no overshoot on type, no glow, no colour gradient as decoration, people only as hands and backs, one style for the whole film, and the film never names a reference.

## Swiss grid
*From lemo-opuscar's swiss-motion. Fits announcements, numbers, rules and systems.*
1. **Essence.** Flat graphic design on a strict grid. One family of type, black and greys, and one signal colour. Everything snaps to the grid and lands on the pace grid; nothing bounces. Huge scale contrast: a giant numeral beside small technical notes. One element breaks the system and becomes the character. Not a keynote, not a lyric video, not a chart.
2. **Material.** Paper, ink and two or three light greys for the grid. Bars and blocks are flat rectangles with mass; fewer, wider modules. A light-grey layer under the black gives depth without a second colour. No shadows, no texture, no grain (finish off or `soft` at most).
3. **Colour logic.** `--ground` and `--text` carry everything. `--accent` belongs to one element only, the protagonist. No `--accent-2`. Black type may sit on the accent.
4. **Words on screen.** One family in several weights (`--disp` or `--body`), flush left, ragged right, on grid lines. Large white space is part of the layout. Small notes sit under a 1 px rule, like a museum label. Words rise out of a clip box; they never fade.
5. **Motion quality.** Use one exact ease for every snap and plain linear time for lines as they are drawn; no third curve. To delete something, draw a hairline across it and let it fold flat onto that line. Ripples run outward from their cause. The exception alone moves linear and off the grid.
6. **Camera grammar.** Hold on a spread (cause left, effect right). Cut or glide one column or page along. Push into one module to show the unit of the system. Pull back to a wall of outputs made by the same rules.
7. **Rhythm without sound.** Fix a pace grid (for example 0.5 s) and land every snap on it. The rhythm is the regular arrival of blocks.
8. **Native moves.** *The grid means something*: columns become hours, floors or years; fits a week of on-call, a hiring plan, a product roadmap. *The exception*: one element goes off grid; fits an outlier result, the one customer who churned, the person who changed the process. *Knife cut*: fits a cut budget, a removed feature, a shortened approval chain. *Re-flow*: everything rearranges around one change, nearest first; fits a reorganised team, a layout for mobile.
9. **Pitfalls.** Many thin columns read as a chart: use fewer, wider modules. Rotating and labelling at once is noise: rotate, hold, then label. A merely tilted layout is not a better one: use one strong diagonal and a larger signal element.
10. **Build in Atelier.** Blocks and rules in the canvas through `ART.film` and `CAM`; every word in HTML on the same grid. Snaps with `MO.smooth` over a short window, no springs with bounce.
11. **Variation space.** Structures: a timetable that fills hour by hour; an alphabet of the topic; two systems side by side until one element crosses the gutter. Openings: one dot placed, the grid growing from it; the finished layout, then taken apart into its rules; a giant numeral that turns out to be a detail. Endings: the empty grid after every block is cut; one word flush left in a corner; the exception leaves and the system closes over its space.

## Whiteboard
*From lemo-opuscar's whiteboard. Fits explainers: how something works, why a number moved.*
1. **Essence.** One glossy dry-erase board, drawn live. The board is a map: each idea has its own region, and distance on the board is distance in the argument. Strokes are written in order, slightly imperfect, with ghosts of old lessons. No hands, no arms, no cursor: markers and magnets move by themselves. Not a blueprint, not kinetic type.
2. **Material.** A flat warm white board with faint scuffs and a few ghost marks at 5 to 10% strength. Marker ink multiplies where strokes cross. Strokes wobble a little along their length, start with a pooled dot and overshoot where a circle closes. Magnets cast a small shadow.
3. **Colour logic.** No more than three markers, and each keeps its meaning for the whole film: `--text` for things and structure, `--text-2` for process or measurement, `--accent` for the one thing that matters, once per region. The board stays neutral.
4. **Words on screen.** The hook and the message stay HTML in `--body` or `--disp`, and render.mjs measures them. The hand face in the font library (Nothing You Could Do) is for one short annotation only, as data/type_pairings.json says; at display size the audit warns of a thin stroke. Labels written on the board are drawn strokes: report them with `window.texts(t)`. A label is wholly in or wholly out of every shot.
5. **Motion quality.** Each stroke runs slow, fast, slow, inside a time window, so writing ends on time. A label takes 0.3 to 0.8 s, a title about 3 s. The marker parks off frame between phrases. The eraser moves at a steady speed and always leaves a ghost.
6. **Camera grammar.** Glide along the line the marker is drawing. Hold to read a definition. Punch into one symbol when it is the point. Pull back to the whole board as the summary. Cut only as a fast glide to a new region.
7. **Rhythm without sound.** The writing itself is the beat: one phrase, one drawing, one move of the camera.
8. **Native moves.** *Writing in order*: the reveal is the explanation; fits a pricing formula built term by term, a funnel's conversion rates. *The marker leads the camera*: fits a request travelling through a system, an approval chain. *The eraser rewinds*: fits "what if we had not", an undo, a rollback. *The ghost becomes relevant*: last year's forecast faint under this year's; fits a revised plan.
9. **Pitfalls.** One marker with too many jobs runs late: give each drawing a deadline and a second marker. A dashed line at the minimum stroke time takes seconds: give dashes their own floor. Labels half cut by a push look careless.
10. **Build in Atelier.** Strokes as point arrays drawn up to a fraction from `MO.smooth`, under `CAM`. Ink with `globalCompositeOperation = "multiply"`. Words in HTML or reported by `window.texts`.
11. **Variation space.** Structures: a proof that fills downward step by step; a debate, two markers on two halves until the drawings meet; a timeline ridden left to right. Openings: a huge question mark that shrinks into the first dot; an erased board with a wrong answer still faintly visible; one magnet clacking down on a clean board. Endings: the answer circled twice and the marker capped; the board wiped except one line; a push into the smallest symbol, which becomes the title.

## Pencil chart
*From lemo-opuscar's dataviz. Fits one real number with a story: growth, a drop, a record. Chart choice and honesty rules: references/dataviz.md.*
1. **Essence.** The film is nothing but the chart. Each mark stands for a real value, and each movement does something a chart does: plot a point, extend an axis, rescale, pin a note. A two-colour pencil draws every mark, and handwritten notes pin a human scale to the numbers. Not a dashboard, not slides with charts.
2. **Material.** Warm paper with a faint dot grid fixed to the paper. Dark ink for axes and series, pale grey for the grid and the zero line. Notes in pencil, on curved leaders ending in an open ring around the mark. Only the tip of the pencil is drawn.
3. **Colour logic.** Colour carries the value and nothing else. `--accent` marks records and warnings, `--accent-2` memories and human notes; a change of lead marks the turn. Keep small values light: a colour ramp is never stretched to dramatise a change.
4. **Words on screen.** The caption is the figure caption: HTML under the chart, two lines at most, with a hairline rule above it. The chart's own headline sits above the plot. Tick labels in `--mono`. Pencil notes are drawn: report them with `window.texts(t)`.
5. **Motion quality.** The pencil lifts before each mark, lands, and rebounds a hair. It hurries as the data gets dense and hesitates before a key mark. A rescale moves in small eased steps. Notes fade while a series morphs and return after.
6. **Camera grammar.** Start close on one mark and follow its leader. Pull back as the axes grow out of the marks. Glide with the pencil, the newest mark about 60% across the frame. Hold locked while a series morphs. Punch in on an outlier.
7. **Rhythm without sound.** The data sets the pace: one mark per beat, and the beat halves to show acceleration.
8. **Native moves.** *One mark, one fact*: fits the first client, the first hire, the first incident. *Axes grow with the story*: the unit arrives when it is first named; fits revenue, headcount, response time. *Breaking the frame*: the value outgrows the chart and the torn edge stays; fits a spike in signups, a cost overrun. *The open slot*: a dashed outline where the next, still unknown value will go; fits next quarter, the next release.
9. **Pitfalls.** Select data columns by name, never by position, and print every number the film claims. Notes collide with later marks: pin them in data space and keep a memory zone for old notes. A frame break that rescales too fast is missed: hold the broken state half a beat.
10. **Build in Atelier.** Marks, axes and pencil in the canvas under `CAM`; the headline and caption in HTML. Values from a local data file, with the source line on screen. Counters with `M.counter` from assets/lib/motion.js (add its script tag: the page in art-motion.md does not load it), settled values reported to `window.texts`.
11. **Variation space.** Structures: a ranked race with one bar as the character; a map of dots that falls into a unit chart of people; a slope chart of two years facing each other. Openings: the complete chart first, then the story unwinds it; the measured thing before any axis (a drawn ticket turns into the first mark); a blank plot holding only its unit and a handwritten question. Endings: one mark, close, with its note; the chart folds back into the object it measured; the pencil strikes a word of the headline and writes the true one.

## Pictogram system
*From lemo-opuscar's pictogram-motion. Fits catalogues and processes: N steps, N tools, N teams.*
1. **Essence.** Three ingredients and nothing more: a square-grid pattern of discs and half-discs in rows that shift sideways against each other, pictograms assembled from round-ended bars and discs, and big heavy type that rises out of masks. Either a catalogue (one card per item, a mono index "07 / 12") or a story told in pictograms. Not isometric and not a chart. Not a cartoon either: a pictogram shows no feelings, and a figure has no face.
2. **Material.** Flat canvas shapes on one square grid (a cell about a sixth of the frame height). The pattern is drawn wider than the frame, so any row can shift by one cell. One stroke weight per film. A pictogram that does not read at 60 px is cut.
3. **Colour logic.** A chapter owns one hue, in tones from the dealt plan: `--accent` and `--accent-2` for two chapters at most, `--ground` and `--text` for the rest. Behind words the pattern is kept faint; a chapter card shows it at full strength. Light grounds take dark type.
4. **Words on screen.** A very heavy title (`--disp` at its boldest), a second line under it, and small mono text in the corners like a broadcast lower third, with a progress row of N ticks. Type slides up out of a mask: title first, the second line 0.08 s later.
5. **Motion quality.** Exponential ease-out for entrances, ease-in-out for wipes. Pattern rows drift in alternate directions and jump a quarter cell on the pace grid. Every transition follows the grid: rows slide, columns fall, or an iris opens from the last pictogram. No dissolves.
6. **Camera grammar.** Mostly a locked frame: the card is a poster. Glide one screen along a track per item. Cut down by grid rows for a ranking. Push into one pattern cell until it becomes the next frame. Pull back to a wall of all the items.
7. **Rhythm without sound.** One card per bar of the pace grid; a sub-family at double speed. The progress ticks make the rhythm visible.
8. **Native moves.** *A card per item*: the total is the hook ("all 12"); fits every step of an onboarding, every tool in a stack. *Chapter is colour*: families become hues; fits departments, phases of a project. *Count-up slam*: a number rolls to its value and slams; fits tonnes saved, hours returned. *Icon wall*: all pictograms return as one grid in one colour; fits a year in review.
9. **Pitfalls.** Light hues swallow light type: dark type on light grounds. A figure crossing the title column: one column for text, one stage for the pictogram. Imitating a real event identity: invent your own pattern motifs, and never borrow an event's pictograms, emblem or colour names.
10. **Build in Atelier.** Pattern and pictograms in the canvas, seeded with `U.rng`; titles, index and ticks in HTML, each title marked `data-read`. Slams with `MO.slam` and the camera's `slam`.
11. **Variation space.** Structures: a countdown from N to 1 that speeds up; a day in pictograms, one card per hour; an assembly, each card adding a part to one machine in the corner. Openings: a single pattern cell fills the screen, then divides into the grid; the first pictogram caught already at work; an empty grid drawn line by line on the pace grid. Endings: the last card held while rows stop one by one; the last pictogram leaves and the ticks run out; the grid comes apart into rows that slide away.

## Riso print
*From lemo-opuscar's risograph. Fits human stories, culture posts, a launch with warmth.*
1. **Essence.** A film that looks printed on a stencil duplicator, frame by frame. Two or three translucent inks on warm paper; where two inks cross they make a third colour. Plates sit a few pixels out of register. Mid-tones are halftone dots on a screen angle per ink. Flat shapes, no outlines. Not vector art with noise on top, not process-colour halftone, not opaque screen print. Never a machine brand or trade dress.
2. **Material.** Each ink is its own layer, multiplied over the paper. No area takes more than two inks, and only one of those may be screened; stacking three screens turns to mud. Large flat shapes and lots of bare paper. Faint ink grain, pinholes in solids and slight streaks along the feed.
3. **Colour logic.** `--accent` and `--accent-2` are the two inks, `--text` an optional third, `--ground` the paper. Every other colour is an overprint. Every main shape contains some of the first ink, so a one-ink frame still reads. Save the full overprint for the moment that earns it.
4. **Words on screen.** HTML in `--disp` or `--body`, on bare paper or on a knockout strip, in `--text`. Never on a halftone field.
5. **Motion quality.** Objects act on twos (`MO.step(t, 12)`), the camera moves on every frame. Registration is fixed per shot, not jittered every frame. An accent kicks the plates apart by a few pixels for a quarter second; a held moment lets them drift and snap back.
6. **Camera grammar.** A wide hold with a small figure crossing. A slow glide sideways for travel or routine. A locked frame (horizon, a disc, one subject) for the film's one pause. Push into a halftone field until the dots are shapes. Pull back to show it was one sheet.
7. **Rhythm without sound.** Plates arrive at turning points: the image gains an ink at each one.
8. **Native moves.** *Plates as story*: the world gains an ink at each turn; fits a team growing, a product arriving. *The overlap colour*: two inks make a new colour only where they cross; fits a merger, a partnership, the finding that sits where two data sets overlap. *Knockout reveal*: a shape made of missing ink, filled later; fits a gap in the market, the missing piece of a plan. *Halftone scale*: dots grow into shapes; fits a crowd that turns out to be customers.
9. **Pitfalls.** Three halftones in one area: two inks, one tint. Registration jitter on every frame flickers and bloats the file: fixed offsets per shot. Saturated dots on skin read as a rash: a light tint of the palest ink.
10. **Build in Atelier.** One offscreen canvas per ink in `init`, drawn with `globalCompositeOperation = "multiply"` and a fixed offset per shot. Halftone as dots on a rotated grid fixed to the paper, not to the objects.
11. **Variation space.** Structures: two inks in dialogue, meeting only at the end; subtraction, losing an ink at each loss; a zine, each shot a page with a new ink pair. Openings: a blank knockout in a full field; extreme close on dots that pull back into an image; three colour ghosts that snap into register. Endings: one ink left; the print pasted on a wall inside the world it showed; the sheet folded into an envelope or a ticket.

## Screen print
*From lemo-opuscar's silkscreen-poster. Fits events, launches, a series of places or products with a few facts.*
1. **Essence.** A poster printed on camera, one opaque ink at a time; each ink covers the one under it. No halftone, no outlines: an edge is where one flat ink stops. Skies and haze are stepped bands, never gradients. Thin slivers of bare paper where a layer slips. Facts arrive in the order a printer would lay them down, a layer per beat. Not riso, not woodcut.
2. **Material.** One shape path per ink. A ragged squeegee edge reveals each pull, with the blade and a bead of ink riding on it. Cream stock with fibres; mottle and pinholes show only on ink. A portrait sheet with a dark info band at the foot, or a full-bleed print.
3. **Colour logic.** Up to six inks by role, from the dealt plan: light sky, deep sky, far, mid, near (the darkest; the info band uses it too), plus a single bright `--accent` kept for the sun, the route or the key number. Values step from light far away to dark near. Bare paper is the brightest thing on the sheet. Text inks are chosen by contrast with the ink beneath.
4. **Words on screen.** A sign face (National Park in the font library) or `--disp` for the name; facts in the info band in `--body`, printed last, when they are read. Each fact is `data-read`.
5. **Motion quality.** A squeegee pull is linear and steady. Facts print left to right on the pace grid. No fade anywhere. Loose layers drift by depth, then snap into register on one hit before the key facts.
6. **Camera grammar.** Glide with the squeegee as colour arrives. Hold top-down on the full sheet while layers stack. Push to the info band to read the facts. Pull back to the wall where the series hangs.
7. **Rhythm without sound.** One ink per beat; the snap into register is the accent.
8. **Native moves.** *One ink, one beat*: facts print last; fits an event's date, venue and time. *Re-ink*: the same poster in a new palette is time passing or a variant; fits three plans or colourways, summer and winter hours. *Register snap*: fits a launch price, a date that matters. *The series wall*: every print hung together at the end; fits four offices, a product family.
9. **Pitfalls.** Too many facts at honest reading times overrun the film: print side by side in the same pull, or leave secondary facts for the final wall. Parallax alone reads as moving mountains: show paper gaps while loose, then snap. A night palette swallows a header: choose text inks by contrast.
10. **Build in Atelier.** `Path2D` per ink in the canvas, clipped by the squeegee edge; the info band and the name in HTML on top, measured by render.mjs and `data-read`.
11. **Variation space.** Structures: one poster, many runs, the band changing each night; a walk down a street where each shop sign gets printed as the camera passes it; a story of misprints, the layers sliding further out until the final pull locks exactly onto the date. Openings: light through a blank screen; the accent first, a lone product on bare paper; the finished wall, then back to the table. Endings: prints on a drying rack; the poster in use on a bus shelter; the squeegee runs dry and leaves a paper silhouette.

## One line
*From lemo-opuscar's one-line. Fits a single idea, a journey, a change over time.*
1. **Essence.** A single unbroken ink line; the pen does not lift once. The whole film is that line in the act of being drawn, and one scene turns into the next instead of cutting. No fills, no second weight, no background art. Only the paper, the line and the soft shadow of the pen. One accent at most, used once. Not a whiteboard, not ink wash, not line art with cuts.
2. **Material.** Warm paper with soft mottling fixed to it. The line is a filled shape, not a plain stroke: thinner when fast, fuller when slow, with a little bleed and a teardrop blot where it stops. The pen itself is never drawn.
3. **Colour logic.** `--text` on `--ground`. `--accent` once, on a stretch of line that means something (danger, money, water); it stays visible in the final picture.
4. **Words on screen.** HTML only, set small and quiet in `--body`, placed where the line is not. A word written by the line is drawn: report it with `window.texts(t)`.
5. **Motion quality.** Every frame, no stepping. Time per point is weighted by curvature: sharp turns take longer. The stroke that matters most tends to be drawn slowest. Going back over a line already drawn is allowed.
6. **Camera grammar.** Glide after the nib with a little lag. Hold composed when a scene must read whole. Push in slowly on a stop. Pull back at the end to show that every scene was one picture.
7. **Rhythm without sound.** Stops are the beats: the nib halts, ink pools, the frame holds for 1.5 to 2.5 s. Keep each stop under 3 s, or keep the camera pushing through it: video_check.py fails a still stretch of 3 s.
8. **Native moves.** *The pen never lifts*: fits a customer journey, a pipeline from lead to invoice. *Transformation*: one picture redraws itself as the next; fits a rough sketch turning into the product, a word turning into the thing it names. *The stop*: when the nib halts, that is the event; fits an outage, a pause before a decision. *Scale reveal*: each small scene turns out to belong to one large picture shown at the close; fits a year's milestones forming the company's mark.
9. **Pitfalls.** Stray connectors read as wrinkles or masks: plan the path so each region is drawn once and connectors fall on natural lines. Test the final picture early, drawn all at once. Lines drawn too close braid together: keep a gap.
10. **Build in Atelier.** The whole film can be one point array, sampled by arc length and revealed up to a fraction of t. Width from speed, a clamp on width during pull-backs, the camera from `CAM`.
11. **Variation space.** Structures: a line that is a graph, each peak a scene; two lines from opposite corners drawn in turn until they meet; a loop that travels back along itself to a starting point that has changed meaning. Openings: the line already racing across the sheet; a complete drawing that comes undone into the story; a handwritten word whose final letter grows into the first scene. Endings: the line leaves the edge of the sheet and does not stop; the pen runs out of ink; the nib hovers, held, before the final stroke.

## Case file
*From lemo-opuscar's halftone-dossier. Fits post-mortems, myth-busting, "the case of the missing pipeline".*
1. **Essence.** The film is a printed case file read one exhibit at a time. Cream archive paper, halftone dots for every shade, large heavy headlines that look printed, case numbers and file tabs, and rubber stamps that slam. One idea per card, held as long as its words need (references/motion.md), usually 3 to 5 seconds. The official tone of the file does the framing, whether the subject is serious or not. Not riso, not a Swiss grid. No characters: exhibits and stamps are the objects.
2. **Material.** Paper with low mottling and a faint fold, built once. There are no gradients: shading is made of halftone dots that grow and shrink with a density map, in two multiplied layers set at different screen angles. Stamps are a double rounded box with rough, mottled ink, tilted a few degrees.
3. **Colour logic.** `--ground` is the paper, `--text` the dark ink for outlines, body text and the label bar. `--accent-2` is the offset shadow, `--accent` is kept for stamps and alarms. Flat inks only.
4. **Words on screen.** One heavy headline per card (`--disp`), flat, with no text-shadow: the audit counts a text-shadow as a glow, so draw any offset print in the canvas under the word. A label bar in the dark ink holds one line about the exhibit; it is not the caption line of captions.json. Case numbers in `--mono`. Words enter by a clip slide, never with overshoot. Each headline and label is `data-read`. A stamp's word is drawn: report it with `window.texts(t)`.
5. **Motion quality.** Objects arrive by sliding up from the frame edge or by a quick scale from zero, with no bounce on words. The stamp slams in about 0.17 s (art-motion.md's `slam`) with one small shake. Transitions are a dot wipe (a grid of circles grows to cover the frame and shrinks into the next card) or a hard cut on a big moment.
6. **Camera grammar.** Hold frontal on the page. Push in slowly before a verdict. Glide across a board of pinned exhibits. Punch into a halftone detail until the dots are huge. Pull back to show the file is one of many.
7. **Rhythm without sound.** Exhibit numbers count the beats; the stamp is the accent, twice a film at most.
8. **Native moves.** *The charge sheet*: the topic is put on file as "the case of X", with its charges listed; fits a bias in hiring, a process that keeps failing. *Numbered exhibits*: a giant halftone numeral behind each beat; fits five causes of an outage. *The stamp*: the verdict lands as a stamp hitting paper; fits APPROVED, SHIPPED, DEPRECATED. *Redaction*: black bars lift one by one; fits a leaked plan, a launch kept secret until today.
9. **Pitfalls.** Fonts not loaded before layout put glyphs in the wrong place: wait for `document.fonts.ready`. Dots under about 14 px step at 1080 px alias after encoding. More than five inks lose the print feel. A dot wipe short of full cover shows the hard switch: grow it past each cell's diagonal.
10. **Build in Atelier.** Paper and halftone cached in `init`, exhibits and stamps in the canvas under `CAM`; headlines and captions in HTML. Slams with `MO.slam` and the camera's `slam` and `shake`.
11. **Variation space.** Structures: a declassified report unredacted page by page; a file read in reverse, starting from the last time anyone saw the subject; a claim where each exhibit contradicts the statement. Openings: one object in an evidence bag on dark ink; a fingerprint in huge dots that resolves as we pull back; a label bar alone on paper before the file drops in. Endings: the file goes into a drawer of identical files; the last page stays redacted; the stamp misses.
