# The brief: the decisions that make a piece authored

A brief is not a description of the deliverable. It is the set of choices that a generic generator would not make. Write it before any layout.

## brief.md fields
| Field | What to write | Bad | Good |
|---|---|---|---|
| one_line | What the viewer should remember, as a sentence they could repeat | "Our Q4 results" | "Twelve steps sit between an order and its invoice; seven are copying." |
| audience | Who, and the moment they meet it | "professionals" | "an operations lead scrolling at 8am between meetings" |
| material idea | "On these pages, [something that belongs to this subject] becomes [the direction's device]." | "Clean modern slides" | "The invoice's own line items become a till receipt." |
| what everyone else does | 2 or 3 typical treatments of this topic in the feed or the competition | | "Stock photos of laptops; a bar chart with a blue gradient" |
| the delta | What only this piece can show, and why it can | | "We have the real step-by-step log from one order." |
| tension | The gap opened at the start and closed at the end: expected vs true, cost vs value, rule vs reality | | "Everyone thinks invoicing is one click. It is twelve steps." |
| one novel element | The single thing the viewer has not seen from you or from the feed | | "The receipt prints itself in the video." |
| direction | The one direction `compose.py deal <slug> genome` deals (references/directions.md) | | "receipt" |
| references | Two real, excellent works from the dealt direction's own world, and the one discipline taken from each. Copy none of their content. | "a Dribbble search for minimal" | "A rail timetable: times aligned on the colon. A bank ledger: a double rule only under the total." |
| format and size | | | "8-page carousel, 1080 x 1350, PDF" |
| must not | What cannot be said or shown (claims, clients, competitors, private data) | | "no client names; numbers only from the log" |

## For art pieces: name the movement first (Anthropic's canvas-design skill)
When the piece is mostly image (a poster, a cover with three words, an abstract key visual), write a visual philosophy before the brief: name the movement in one or two words ("Ledger Silence", "Dispatch Modernism"), then four short paragraphs on how it lives in space and form, colour and material, scale and rhythm, and hierarchy. Keep the subject as a quiet reference inside the work, recognisable to those who know it, never announced. Then write the brief below so the philosophy has a job.

## The swap test (taste-skill)
Put a competitor's name in the one_line and the material idea. If both still work, the piece is generic: its idea belongs to the category, not to this subject. Fix it with something only this subject has: a real number, a real artefact (a log, a form, a ledger, a config line), a real place, a real failure, a real limit.

## Most Advanced Yet Acceptable
People prefer designs that are both typical and novel (Hekkert, Snelders and van Wieringen 2003, the MAYA principle named by Raymond Loewy). Typicality alone is dull; novelty alone is confusing; they suppress each other, so the best work holds both. In practice: keep about 80% of a piece familiar to its audience (a clear structure, a known format, the brand's palette and type) and spend the novelty in ONE place, named in the brief. Two novel elements compete; three are noise.

## The material idea, more examples
- A cost story becomes a receipt or a ledger: the itemised lines are the content.
- A process becomes a departure board, a transit map or a kitchen ticket rail.
- A rule change becomes a code diff or a maintenance task card.
- A definition becomes a library index card or a dictionary entry.
- A forecast or simulation becomes a weather console.
- An overnight automation becomes a building at night with rooms going dark.
- A single big number becomes a Swiss poster.
- A messy legacy tool becomes a museum specimen with a catalogue label.
The test: does the device carry information, or only decorate? If you removed the words, would the device still say something true about the subject?

## Brief red flags
- The one_line uses abstract nouns (solutions, innovation, journey, transformation).
- The material idea names a style ("sleek", "premium", "bold") instead of a thing.
- "What everyone else does" is empty: you have not looked.
- The novel element is an effect (glow, 3D, particles) rather than an idea.
